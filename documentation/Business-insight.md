# Business Insights

## Executive Summary

This project analyzes Indonesia's socioeconomic conditions between 2020 and 2025 using provincial-level data on poverty, unemployment, population, and GDP per capita.

The goal is to identify regional disparities, evaluate long-term trends, and uncover insights that can support evidence-based decision-making.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Poverty Rate Peaked in 2024

Indonesia's average poverty rate increased from 9.2% in 2020 to a peak of 10.8% in 2024 before declining to 8.7% in 2025.

This indicates that poverty reduction was not consistently linear and may have been influenced by economic and regional factors.

---

### 2. Unemployment Improved Significantly

The national unemployment rate decreased from 5.9% in 2020 to 4.5% in 2025.

This suggests a gradual recovery of labor market conditions and improved workforce participation across provinces.

---

### 3. Papua Region Continues to Face Structural Challenges

Papua Pegunungan, Papua Tengah, Papua, Papua Barat, and Papua Selatan consistently recorded the highest poverty rates in Indonesia.

Despite economic development initiatives, substantial socioeconomic disparities remain between eastern and western Indonesia.

---

### 4. High GDP Does Not Always Translate to Low Poverty

Several provinces with high GDP per capita still experienced relatively high poverty rates.

This suggests that economic growth alone is insufficient to reduce poverty without equitable distribution of income and development opportunities.

---

### 5. Papua Achieved the Largest Poverty Reduction

Papua reduced its poverty rate from 26.64% in 2020 to 19.16% in 2025.

This represents the largest improvement among all provinces and demonstrates measurable progress despite remaining one of the highest-poverty regions.

---

## Recommendations

### Strengthen Poverty Reduction Programs

Prioritize targeted poverty alleviation programs in eastern Indonesia, particularly in Papua and surrounding provinces.

### Improve Employment Opportunities

Expand workforce development and employment programs in provinces with persistently high unemployment rates.

### Promote Inclusive Economic Growth

Focus on ensuring that economic growth benefits a broader portion of the population rather than relying solely on GDP expansion.

### Increase Human Capital Investment

Invest in education, healthcare, and infrastructure to improve long-term socioeconomic outcomes.

### Enhance Data-Driven Governance

Regularly monitor provincial performance using data analytics and evidence-based policy evaluation.

---

## Conclusion

While Indonesia experienced improvements in unemployment and poverty reduction during the study period, significant regional disparities remain.

The findings highlight the importance of inclusive development strategies that address structural challenges in underserved regions while promoting sustainable economic growth across the country.
